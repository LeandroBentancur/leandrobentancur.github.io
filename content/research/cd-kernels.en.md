The Christoffel–Darboux (CD) function, obtained from the CD kernel, is a central tool in
approximation theory, orthogonal polynomials and moment-based methods. It efficiently
encodes geometric and statistical information about a measure, with applications to
support inference, anomaly detection, density approximation, optimal sampling and machine
learning.

Together with Mauricio Velasco and Didier Henrion, we introduced mollified CD kernels on
algebraic varieties. They improve support recovery and yield convergence rates for density
recovery without requiring knowledge of the equilibrium measure of the support, which the
classical method needs.
